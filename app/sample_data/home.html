U.S. Strikes Iran Again as Peace Talks Hang by a Thread

The United States military carried out fresh "self-defense" strikes overnight Monday into Tuesday, targeting Iranian missile launch sites and boats attempting to lay mines around the Strait of Hormuz — the latest jolt to a weeks-old ceasefire that both sides are still nominally trying to preserve.

U.S. Central Command confirmed the action early Tuesday, saying American forces acted "to protect our troops from threats posed by Iranian forces." CENTCOM spokesman Tim Hawkins said targets included missile launch facilities in the Bandar Abbas area and Iranian vessels trying to emplace mines in the strategically vital waterway. Iranian state television separately reported blasts around Bandar Abbas, a city home to a major military port on the strait. A pro-government Iranian news outlet reported four Revolutionary Guard troops were killed in the strikes on boats.

The attack came just hours after Iranian negotiators had arrived in Doha for talks with Qatari mediators coordinated with Washington — a positive diplomatic signal that was quickly overshadowed. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, traveling in India, acknowledged the collision of diplomacy and firepower, saying talks could take "a couple of days" to resolve and that the Strait "has to be open — one way or the other." Fox News, citing senior U.S. officials, reported the Iran deal was "95% there," though key disputes over language on Iran's nuclear program and the lifting of sanctions have stalled finalization.

President Trump, speaking on Memorial Day at Arlington National Cemetery, reiterated that Tehran "will never have a nuclear weapon" and insisted any deal must be a "great and meaningful" one — or the U.S. would return to active combat "bigger and stronger than ever before." His administration is pushing a two-phase framework: first reopening the Strait, then tackling the nuclear file over a 30-to-60-day period.

Iranian state-linked media framed Tuesday's strikes as a ceasefire violation. Iran has not officially commented on the latest action, but a senior Iranian official told state media there "will be no retreat" on the nuclear issue. Markets, however, continued to price in a deal: oil futures dropped more than five percent on optimism that the Strait could soon reopen, even as the exchange of fire reinforced how fragile the path to peace remains.
